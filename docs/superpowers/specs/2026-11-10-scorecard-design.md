# Design: scorecard and indicators (Check-in 3, step 2)

*Approved 10 Nov 2026 (builder check done). Branch: `scorecard`. Build plan:
`docs/superpowers/plans/2026-11-10-scorecard.md`. Choices marked **(Claude's call)** were settled after the builder check without the user;

## 1. Intent and why

Brief §5 "Scorecard and indicators" (`reference/01_Student_Brief.md`) requires scores at account, domain and platform
level with formulas, weights, aggregation rules and a sensitivity check; evaluable coverage beside every score "so
missing evidence cannot silently improve a result"; and at least six indicators, at least three leading, each marked
computed, simulated or proposed, with at least three computed from this release (one of them leading). Proposed
indicators name the inputs they need. App. B (illustrative indicator rows) sets the expected level of detail:
calculation, refresh cadence, threshold, owner, decision. Check-in 3 (brief §8) lists "scores, impact and coverage".

The checks are done (D-066, D-067, D-068): every rule publishes affected, eligible, not-evaluable and population
counts in `outputs/02_defect_register.csv`, with failing records in `outputs/02_finding_members.csv`. What is missing
is the layer that turns those into scores a reader can compare, and the indicators that say what is coming. This
session adds that layer. It adds no new checks (D-066) except the two rules behind KPI 1 (§5.10), which the user
chose to code so every Check-in 1 KPI but one has a real value.

The scorecard has to survive the judges' App. D pressure tests:
- "If two accounts both score 85, do they have the same problems, and if not, what does the score hide?" Every score
  carries its per-domain breakdown and its coverage.
- "Which of your leading indicators would have fired before the largest defect you found, and how do you know?" A
  date-ordered look-back (§5.6) answers it with records, not argument.
- "Which of your indicators could an operations team game?" Every indicator has a gaming note.

Research behind the method (10 Nov): there is no industry-standard data-quality scorecard (Datafold), so our formulas
are ours to defend. The OECD/JRC *Handbook on Constructing Composite Indicators* treats weights as trade-offs and
asks for a robustness and sensitivity step that varies weights and aggregation and reports whether rankings move.
Coverage is a meta-dimension: a perfect score on a small share of the data is meaningless. A KPI needs a goal, target,
owner and cadence; pair each leading indicator with the lagging one it should precede.

## 2. Goals, non-goals, anti-goals

**Goals**
- Rule, domain, account and platform scores with coverage, a "confirmed-only" bound and three sensitivity variants,
  all regenerated by the deliverable notebook on the full release.
- Eight indicators in an indicator catalog, six computed, with daily series for the leading ones and a look-back.
- Weights, line mapping and indicator thresholds in `config/`, changeable live by a judge.
- New outputs loaded into `outputs/store.duckdb` by `scripts/build_store.py`, so step 4 (executive view) can use them.

**Non-goals**
- The urgency ranking of defects (exposure × accounts × fix cost, owner, recommended action). It waits for step 4 and
  will read from these scores. It needs its own decision because it mixes dollars, counts and fix cost (D-050).
- Any dashboard page or React code. The store gets the tables; the page comes in step 4.
- New checks or rules beyond the two of §5.10. `ACC_REQUIRED_FIELDS`, `SEC_IDENTIFIER_VALID`,
  `NAV_SHARE_CLASS_MAPPING`, `NAV_OPEN_ISSUES_BEFORE_RELEASE` and `CA_ALL_HOLDERS_ADJUSTED` stay uncoded (D-066).
- The optional predictive risk indicator (brief §5): no train/test model.
- Slides and report text (teammates; numbers come from `outputs/` and `docs/FINDINGS.md`).

**Anti-goals: a failure even if it runs**
- Missing evidence raising a score: a not-evaluable record, an empty domain or an unpriced holding making anything look
  better (RULES.md "Missing data": untestable never raises a score).
- An unresolved finding silently counted as clean.
- Unknown dollars treated as zero, or dollars mixed into a score.
- A weight, threshold or line mapping hard-coded in the notebook.
- Thresholds tuned on this release's own results. Indicator thresholds come from App. B, our Check-in 1 KPI targets
  (`scripts/framework.json`) or are written as team assumptions before the values are looked at.
- A leading label claimed as predictive accuracy (App. B: a leading indicator does not establish predictive accuracy
  without a time-ordered test).
- Any existing register line or existing output number moving (new lines are added at the end only, §5.10).

### 5.2 Tests, rule and domain scores

- **Tests (user, after the builder check).** The register's lines under one rule often count different things (e.g.
  `PRC_QUOTE_ELIGIBLE`: position rows for coverage, securities for freshness, security-dates for the preferred
  vendor; `TXN_DUPLICATE`: rows and transaction_ids; `TXN_TIMESTAMPS_IN_ORDER`: transaction_ids and feed-days), and
  `02_finding_members.csv` is sometimes coarser than a line's unit (a security ID standing for many position rows).
  So each rule is split into **tests**, each counting one unit over one population. Which lines form which test is in
  the config table (§5.3).
- **Per-record outcomes.** For every test, the notebook builds one outcome per unit (pass / fail-confirmed /
  fail-unresolved / cleared / not evaluable) from its own frames, not from the members file. A unit failing two lines
  of one test counts once. This outcome table is also what the account scores (§5.4) and step 4's drill-down read.
  Its totals must reconcile with the register: for each counted line, its failing units equal the line's `affected`
  (asserted), and each test's population equals the population of its lines.
- **Test score** = (passed + unresolved-only failures × unresolved credit) ÷ evaluable, scaled to 0–100.
  The credit is 0.5 under D-073. **Test coverage** = evaluable ÷ population.
- **Rule score** = average of its test scores (equal weights in config); **rule coverage** = average of its
  tests' coverage.
- **Domain score** = Σ wᵣ · rule scoreᵣ ÷ Σ wᵣ over the domain's rules that have at least one evaluable unit.
  **Domain coverage** = Σ wᵣ · coverageᵣ ÷ Σ wᵣ over *all* the domain's rules (a rule with nothing evaluable adds 0).
- **Platform score** = Σ w_d · domain score_d ÷ Σ w_d over domains with a score. **Platform coverage** = Σ w_d ·
  coverage_d ÷ Σ w_d over all seven domains.
- **Weights:** equal at both levels to start, in config. A rule on a small table and one on the largest table count the same, so serious
  checks on small tables are not drowned out.
- **Scale:** 0–100, two decimals rounded down (superseded during the build; see §8 "Score rounding").

### 5.3 Evidence status

- Confirmed and unresolved findings both retain their failure labels and counts. In the main score, confirmed
  failures earn zero and unresolved-only failures earn half-credit (D-073). Cleared findings get full credit.
- The **confirmed-only** comparison applies the existing policy: only confirmed failures lose credit, and any
  independent reason a unit cannot be evaluated still applies.
- Lines that RULES.md says are "reported beside" a measure (e.g. holdings priced only by a fallback vendor, flat
  price runs) are not fails. Where RULES.md "Missing data" says a case is "reported separately" (e.g. a holding on
  only one side for custodian agreement), it is not evaluable for that measure: it lowers coverage, never the score.
- Which register line feeds which domain, rule and test, and with what role, is a table in config that a judge can
  read and change. A line not in the table makes the run fail (no silent drop). Roles:
  - **fail:** its units are flagged as confirmed or unresolved failures, with scoring credit as defined above;
  - **cleared:** its units pass;
  - **beside:** reported next to the test, no effect on it;
  - **not evaluable:** its units leave the test's evaluable count (lower coverage, never the score);
  - **summary (Claude's call):** a total whose breakdown lines are also in the table (e.g. "Vendor quotes on the
    snapshot date: spread above threshold" over the "Spread flag: …" lines; "Repeated transaction_id in the pipeline"
    over the three repeat lines; "Held security with no price at all" beside "Holding with no eligible quote"). It is
    not scored, so its breakdown decides, and a cleared breakdown really does count as a pass. The run asserts that a
    summary's units equal the union of its breakdowns' units where the register says they should.

### 5.4 Account scores

- An account's score uses the same rules, restricted to that account:
  - **own records:** its trades (Trades rules), its positions keys (Positions rules), a position on an account ID
    missing from `accounts` (Accounts);
  - **inherited through holdings:** each snapshot position row on a security that is unmastered (Securities),
    unpriced, stale or vendor-flagged (Prices), or under a quarantined or unresolved corporate action (Corporate
    actions) counts as a fail for that account.
- The fund's NAV arithmetic result is shown **beside** the account (via fund and share class), never inside its
  score, so one fund failure does not hit tens of thousands of accounts identically.
- Each account row shows its per-domain scores and coverage, so two accounts at 85 can be told apart.
- Every account ID that appears anywhere is scored, including IDs absent from the account master (they are the
  Accounts domain's fails).
- **Rules with no account link are left out of account scores (Claude's call):** FX rates, the feed-day latency
  medians, vendor flags on securities nobody holds, corporate-action term lines with no holders. They are "not
  applicable" to an account, not "not evaluable", so they don't lower every account's coverage. An account's score
  rolls up over six domains (NAV is beside it).
- **Unit for inherited fails (Claude's call):** the account's snapshot position rows for the Securities and Prices
  tests (a security-level result is copied onto every snapshot row holding that security). Corporate actions use
  one unit per action and holder key (next bullet).
- **Corporate actions inherited (Claude's call; superseded during the build, see §8):** holders are the candidate holders `02_ca_readiness.csv` already
  computes (source security on the ex date). An action whose `verdict` is quarantined is a fail in both scores; an
  action that only carries an unresolved flag (extra terms, ex date after effective date) gets partial credit in the
  main score and full credit in the confirmed-only score, consistent with §5.3. Every action's effective date is before the snapshot
  in this release; an action effective after the snapshot would not be inherited.

### 5.5 Sensitivity check

Three variants, each reported against the main score:

1. **NAV-proximity weights:** domains closer to the NAV weigh more (Positions, Prices, NAV), values in config.
2. **Weakest link:** domain score = its lowest rule score. The platform score is the lowest domain score.
3. **Confirmed-only bound** (§5.3).

For each variant: platform and domain scores and their change from main; for accounts, how many of the N worst
accounts stay in the worst N (N in config) and the rank correlation across all scored accounts.

note (how an operations team could make the number look better without fixing anything, and what would show it).

**Indicator 2 (D-074):** replay alerts using the eligible per-ID timestamps from notebook section 9b. A trade
triggers once if the alert instant comes strictly before both booking and cutoff. The series counts alerts
per feed and cutoff day, and the headline counts alerted trades across the release. The older feed-wide
median remains available with its four-hour limit in the timeliness diagnostics.

**Indicator 1:** the daily series of the security-existence part of `TXN_SECURITY_NOT_MASTERED`, coded in §5.10
(at first an indicator-only exception to D-066; superseded when the user chose to code the rule).

**Look-back (retrospective, not predictive accuracy; method is Claude's call after the builder check):** for each
computed leading indicator and the lagging problem it should precede:

- the leading side's first breach date and the first alert per key. I2 uses the exact alert timestamp for each trade;
- the lagging side's date: the snapshot for snapshot-only problems (positions, prices, NAV), stated as such, since
  the release has no earlier book. I2 uses the cutoff timestamp for each late booking;
- **the evidence:** the share of the lagging problem's keys that the leading indicator flagged earlier (e.g. "k of n
  unpriced securities were already on unmastered-security trades, first seen d days before the snapshot"), with the
  earliest and median lead. A lagging key with no earlier leading flag is listed as a miss. A pair with no shared key
  says "timing only" and claims no lead.

Because a snapshot-only lagging date makes any lead positive by construction, the claim rests on the shared-key share
and the misses, not on the lead time. Pairs:

| Leading | Lagging problem it should precede | Shared by |
|---|---|---|
| Unmastered security rate | positions on unmastered securities / held securities with no price at the snapshot | security_id |
| Pre-cutoff booking alerts | trades booked after cutoff | transaction_id |
| Corporate action readiness | position breaks at the snapshot on the action's source or target security | security_id |
| Fresh, agreed price coverage | snapshot holdings with no eligible or a flagged quote | security_id |

### 5.10 The two rules behind KPI 1 (user, 10 Nov, an exception to D-066)

Coded in the deliverable notebook's check sections (section 9c, beside the other trade checks), not in the scorecard
section, so the register stays the single list of findings. Unit: one transaction row, on the same population the
other `TXN_FIELDS_VALID` lines use. The accounts table has `status` (OPEN / CLOSED), `open_date` and `close_date`;
transactions carry `cusip` and `currency`.

| Rule (RULES.md) | Domain | Tests | Fails when |
|---|---|---|---|
| `TXN_SECURITY_NOT_MASTERED` (§2) | Securities | existence; cusip; currency | security_id not in `securities` (a blank security_id is a fail, RULES.md "Missing data"); trade cusip ≠ master cusip; trade currency ≠ master currency. Cusip and currency are not evaluable when the security is unmastered or a side is blank |
| `ACC_NOT_OPEN_ON_TRADE_DATE` (§1) | Accounts | existence; open on the trade date | account_id not in `accounts`; trade_date before `open_date` or after a filled `close_date` |

- **Status is today's, dates are history (Claude's call):** `status` is the account's status at extract time, so an
  account now CLOSED whose `close_date` is on or after the trade date was open when it traded. Judged by the dates;
  such trades get their own **cleared** register line (a look-alike: "Trade on an account closed later, open on the
  trade date"). An account marked OPEN with a filled `close_date`, or CLOSED with none, is a confirmed line of its own
  and not evaluable for the open-date test. RULES.md is reworded to match.
- **Not evaluable:** an unreadable trade date (open-date test only); an unknown account is a fail of the existence
  test and not evaluable for the open-date test (RULES.md "Missing data": reported separately).
- **Register lines** (status confirmed unless marked; final names are the builder's): trade on a security_id missing
  from `securities`; trade cusip differs from the master; trade currency differs from the master; trade on an
  account_id missing from `accounts`; trade before the account's open_date; trade after the account's close_date;
  the cleared look-alike above; account status contradicts its close_date. Plain wording in
  `scripts/plain_language.json`; members in `02_finding_members.csv`.
- **Tests:** fixture cases for each line (including the closed-later look-alike, a blank security_id and a DD/MM
  trade date), shuffle invariance, and an independent DuckDB recount of the existence counts on the raw files.

## 8. Decisions taken while building

- **A sixth `master_checks` column, `status_check` (Task 1, D-069).** K8 needs its own not-evaluable set (unknown
  account only). `open_check` cannot give it, because it is also not evaluable on unreadable dates. `open_check` keeps
  the value `status contradicts`, and K5–K7 treat it as not evaluable.
- **A status other than OPEN or CLOSED (Task 1).** An account whose status is neither (e.g. blank) is judged on its
  dates alone and is never on K8. The real data has only OPEN and CLOSED.
- **A repeated master key with differing rows (Task 1).** A `security_id` or `account_id` held on more than one master
  row with differing values still passes existence (K1, K4) but is not evaluable on K2, K3 and K5–K7. `unique_master`
  never guesses which master row is right; identical repeats count once.
- **Score rounding (plan ruling, all tasks).** Scores and coverage are on a 0–100 scale, rounded down to two decimals
  (`floor(x * 100) / 100`) and written with `'{:.2f}'`. §5.2 said one decimal, but one decimal could print 99.97 as
  100.0, and a score with any failing unit must never print as 100. A blank score means "nothing evaluable", never 0.
- **A line's population as units (Task 2).** Besides `units` and `ne_units`, `finding()` takes `scope`: the units a
  line looks at, where that is not every unit of its kind (e.g. rebooks, rows with a correction link, flagged
  securities, snapshot position rows). A test's population is the union of its scored and summary lines' scopes
  (`TEST_UNITS`), and the CHECK asserts each line's scope size equals its register `population`. A second CHECK
  asserts each test's population equals the largest register `population` among its lines that are not beside (final
  review C1: the vendor summary lines look at every held security, their flag lines only at the flagged ones, so
  `PRC.vendors` had shrunk to the flagged securities and read 100% coverage although some held securities cannot be
  compared). For transaction_id lines the scope
  is always given, because `timing` and `economic` hold different IDs.
- **The evidence status decides fail or cleared (Task 2).** On a line mapped as `fail` or `cleared`, a register status
  of `cleared` makes it cleared and any other status makes it fail; the run prints any line where this differs from
  the config role. Today no line differs. A line whose evidence changes (e.g. "Closed position not reported" turning
  unresolved on other data) then counts as an unresolved failure without a config edit. Its score credit follows §5.3.
- **Feed-day units (Task 2).** The feed-day unit is the feed and day (`FEED YYYY-MM-DD`), not the timeliness row, so a
  feed-day judged on both feed and processing latency is one unit, and the population is the register's 90.
- **Copies of one transaction_id are separate transaction-row units (Task 2).** On `transaction row` tests every row is
  a unit, so two exact copies give two identical rows in `02_score_outcomes.csv` (both carry the same content ID). In
  memory `outcomes` keeps the unit's index label (`unit`), and the CHECK counts distinct (test, unit) pairs (R2).
- **Accounts on units (Task 2).** A transaction_id unit gets one row per account its copies name; a blank account only
  where no copy names one (e.g. an ID seen only in the pipeline). Security, action, feed-day, ledger, rate and price
  units carry a blank `account_id` in `02_score_outcomes.csv`; account attribution of securities and actions is Task 4.
  Rate-row and price-row IDs are the raw text of their fields (`currency rate_date rate_to_usd`,
  `security_id price_date vendor`).
- **Mapping errors (Task 2).** An unmatched register line raises `KeyError(name)`; a line matched by two exact entries,
  or by two prefixes of the same length, raises `ValueError`. Both stop the run.
- **Summary lines are checked against their breakdowns (Task 2, §5.3).** A summary line passes its `units` but is never
  scored. Its `[[lines]]` entry names its breakdown lines: `covers` (lines whose units all belong to the summary) and
  `shares` (lines that may also hold another summary's units), with `relation = "equals"` or `"subset"`. The CHECK
  compares the summary's units with the union of the breakdown lines' marked and not-evaluable units; a summary entry
  without `covers` or a relation stops the run. Each vendor-quote summary equals its flag's odd and backed lines plus
  its share of "Spread or scale flag: no two vendors agree" (which spans both flags), so `shares` holds that line.
  "Held security with no price at all" is a subset of "Holding with no eligible quote on the snapshot date" (that
  line also holds securities quoted on other dates only). Security units of the vendor lines are `price_quality`
  labels; the position-row summary uses `positions` labels. A summary line also gives its `scope` and `ne_units`
  (final review C1): its scope joins the test's population and its not-evaluable units are marked not evaluable in
  the test, but its marked units are never scored, so the breakdown lines still decide every fail and clear. The
  outcome table's `line` then names the first such summary line for those units.

- **Score functions (Task 3).** `floor2` adds 1e-9 before flooring, so a float product just under a whole cent
  (0.57 * 100 = 56.99999999999999) does not lose a cent. Scores stay unrounded through the roll-up and are floored only
  when written. `test_counts`, `rule_scores`, `domain_scores` and `platform_scores` take an optional group column

  domain, and one with no rule that has a score gets a blank score and coverage 0. `rule_scores` carries the rule's
  weight (config `[weights.rules]`, else 1) in a `weight` column that `domain_scores` reads.
- **Failed counts on a test row (Task 3).** `failed_confirmed` counts units that fail the main outcome and the

- **Sensitivity (Task 5).**
  - **Change from written values:** `change` is the variant's written score minus the main written score (both
    rounded down to two decimals), so the file adds up. It is blank when either score is blank.
  - **Domain rows of `nav_proximity`:** they equal the main domain scores (domain weights only move the platform
    and account scores). They are still written, so the file has the same 27 rows for every variant.
  - **Accounts use the same variants on their own domains:** `nav_proximity` takes the six non-NAV entries of
    `[weights.nav_proximity]`; `weakest_link` takes the worst rule score per domain and the worst domain per account,
    over the rules and domains that reach it. `confirmed_only` is the account's confirmed-only score.
  - **Worst N and correlation:** both use the written (rounded-down) scores, over the accounts scored in both. Ties
    in the N lowest break by account_id. With fewer common accounts than N, all of them are kept. The correlation is
    written with four decimals. Spearman is computed as the Pearson correlation of average ranks, because pandas'
    `method='spearman'` needs scipy (not a project dependency).
  - **Ties at the cutoff (final review T5):** `ties_at_cutoff`, after `worst_n_kept` on the accounts rows, counts the
    accounts that share the variant's N-th lowest written score. Under weakest link and confirmed-only many accounts
    share a floored score (a weakest link is often a rule at 0.00), so the N lowest are then picked by account_id
    among the tied ones and `worst_n_kept` reflects that tie-break; the notebook text says to read it with this count
